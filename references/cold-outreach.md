# Cold Outreach: how to write one

Read this before drafting any prospect email. It captures the approved standard, the rules behind it, and the steps to follow so the next draft matches this quality.

## Sources to read first

1. `references/voice.md`: Son's register. Match it, and never send external copy without showing a draft first.
2. `context/about-business.md` and `context/about-me.md`: what Son offers (AI consulting and implementation for businesses adopting AI).
3. `context/priorities.md`: the 90-day goals (first client; document client work and post it).
4. `.agents/skills/marketing skills/skills/cold-email/SKILL.md`, plus `references/personalization.md`, `references/subject-lines.md`, and `references/follow-up-sequences.md` in the same skill folder.
5. The prospect row in the SunAii Database sheet: business name, website, the signal that made them a prospect, and the signal date.

## Steps

1. **Find a real, recent signal** (job posting, funding, announcement, post). Note the date. If it is older than about 60 days or the role may be filled, verify it or pick a different prospect.
2. **Find the right person** (hiring manager or operations leader) and **verify the email** before sending. Never guess addresses.
3. **Write the email** using the rules below.
4. **Read it aloud and check against the quality bar.** If it could be sent by a sales tool to a hundred companies, rewrite it.
5. **Show the draft to Son for approval.** Do not send anything yourself.

## Rules

- **Subject:** 2 to 4 words, lowercase, internal-looking (e.g., "ops role", "ai ops hiring"). No first name, no product pitch, no emojis, no urgency.
- **Length:** under about 60 words in the body.
- **Opening:** name one specific, true thing about them. Not "I hope this finds you well" or "I came across your profile."
- **Middle:** one useful observation or question tied to their situation. No feature list, no pitch of Son's services.
- **Ask:** one low-friction question. Do not ask for a meeting in the first touch.
- **Sign-off:** "Son" (matching the voice samples). Add an opt-out line such as "Reply STOP and I won't email again" only if the email could read as unsolicited.
- **Tone:** a peer who noticed something, not a vendor. Contractions are fine. Avoid "leverage," "synergy," "best-in-class," and em dashes.
- **Failure check:** if the email reads as "let me sell you something," it fails. Son's feedback: a version that offered a call sounded like a sales pitch. A version that asked a genuine question about their process, with no offer, was accepted.

## Approved example (Prospect: Lakeshore Management, Skokie, IL)

Source signal: posting for "AI-Driven Operations Transformation Lead" (date not verified; confirm before sending).

**Subject:** ops role

Hi, I'm Son.

Saw Lakeshore is hiring for AI-driven operations. Congrats on the push.

Curious, which process is eating the most time for your team right now? I've been looking at where AI actually saves hours versus where it just adds a new tool to manage, and I'm happy to share what I've learned if it's useful.

Son

Status: approved by Son as the tone and structure to match. Not sent. Contact name and email still needed.

## Follow-ups (when a reply is not received)

Follow the cold-email cadence: day 3, day 7, day 14, and a breakup at about day 21 to 28. Each follow-up must add one new useful thing (an insight, a resource, or a relevant example) and never say "just checking in." Stop immediately on any reply, bounce, or STOP request. Draft each follow-up for approval before sending.
